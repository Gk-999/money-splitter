Shared pot & kitty calculator: settle a common fund

Updated 12 November 2026

A shared pot (also called a kitty or common fund) is when everyone chips in a fixed amount up front, say ₹1,000 each, and one person holds the money and pays for things. At the end, money is either left over or the pot ran short. PantherSplits works out exactly who gets a refund and who tops up, for free and without signing up.

Quick answer: each person's fair share is their portion of every item they used. Compare it with what they put in: if they put in more, they get the difference back from the pot holder; if less, they top up the difference to the pot holder.

If the pot runs out

Say the same team had spent ₹6,000 on things everyone shared. That's ₹1,200 each, but everyone only put in ₹1,000, and Meera covered the extra ₹1,000 herself. Each of the other four tops up ₹200 to Meera.

How to run a shared pot with PantherSplits

  1. Switch to Shared Pot and add everyone.
  2. Set "Everyone puts in" to the agreed amount. Change any one person's amount if they put in more or less.
  3. Tap the ★ next to the person holding the money.
  4. Log what was spent from the pot, and untick anyone who wasn't part of an item.
  5. Settle up. The banner shows how much is left or how far over budget you are, and settle-up lists every refund or top-up.

When to use a shared pot

  • Group trips where one person handles all the bookings and bills
  • Office celebrations, farewell gifts and team lunches
  • Monthly household kitties for groceries and help
  • Sports teams, clubs and events with a common fund

If people pay for different things as they go instead, the Split mode fits better.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to leftover money in a kitty?

It goes back to the people who put it in, based on what each person actually used. Someone who skipped an item gets more back than someone who shared it. The person holding the pot pays out the refunds.

What if the kitty runs out of money?

Whoever covered the extra spending is owed it. If the person holding the pot paid the extra from their own money, everyone else tops up their share of the overspend to that person.

Can people put different amounts into the pot?

Yes. Set one amount for everyone, then change any person's amount individually. Each person's refund or top-up is worked out from what they actually put in and what they actually used.
